The Night of All Nights

The Full Moon of Kārtik

Of all the full moons in the year, the full moon of Kārtik — Karthik Purnima, the full moon of Autumn — shines with a singular brilliance. The monsoons have cleared, and the sky is at its most transparent.

In the Vedic tradition, this is not simply an astronomical event. The Srimad Bhagavatam describes how Lord Krishna, hearing this moon rise over Vrindavan, lifted His flute and played a song that called only to the gopis — a call that drew them through forests to the Yamuna's banks.

"On that autumn night, the moon rose, cooling the forest with its rays like the face of Lakshmidevi. The lotuses bloomed as if smiling. The Lord chose to perform His most intimate pastime — the Rāsa dance." — Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 10.29.1–3

The Supreme Pastime

The Rāsa Līlā of Vrindavan

When Krishna played His flute on the night of Karthik Purnima, the gopis felt a longing unlike any worldly desire. They left their homes, their families, their duties — and ran through the moonlit forest toward the Yamuna.

Krishna first sent them back, testing the purity of their love. He told them their duties lay at home. But the gopis replied with a wisdom born of pure devotion: "Our only duty is to You. All dharma finds its fulfillment at Your lotus feet."

"The Supreme Lord, who removes the distress of those who surrender to Him, danced with the gopis in the same way a child plays with his reflection in a mirror." — Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 10.33.3

Then the dance began. Krishna expanded Himself so that each gopi danced with Him alone. The dance lasted through the entire night, yet when it ended, the gopis felt it had passed in a single breath. This is the Rāsa Līlā — the supreme teaching on selfless love.

The Sacred Tradition

Moonlit Kheer — Amrita from the Sky

On the night of Karthik Purnima, the moon is said to pour amrita — divine nectar — along with its light. The ancient tradition is to prepare kheer (rice pudding in milk and sugar) and place it outdoors under the open sky for the entire night.

In the morning, this kheer is offered to Krishna and distributed as prasadam. Those who receive it with faith are said to be blessed with health and spiritual merit.

  • Fresh kheer prepared on the day of Purnima — milk, rice, sugar, saffron
  • Offered to the Lord at sunset with prayers and kirtan
  • Kept under the open sky all night, bathed in moonlight
  • Distributed as prasadam to all in the morning
